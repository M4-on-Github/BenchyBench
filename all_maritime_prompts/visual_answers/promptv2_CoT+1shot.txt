Analyze the provided image of a vessel. Work through your reasoning step by step
before producing the final structured output. Keep each reasoning step to roughly
2–4 sentences.

Step 1 — Observe. Describe what you literally see: the vessel, its orientation,
the water, surroundings, lighting, and any visible damage, smoke, or anomalies.
Do not interpret yet — only catalog what is visible.

Step 2 — Determine state. Apply these rules IN ORDER. State which rule matches,
why, and which earlier rules you ruled out (the rules are priority-ordered, so an
earlier match wins even if a later one also seems to apply):
  1. Resting on or touching seabed/shore/reef/rocks (not floating) but not mostly
     submerged? → aground
  2. Else, overturned, upside down, or severely listing/tilted? → capsized
  3. Else, active flames or significant smoke? → on_fire
  4. Else, completely or mostly underwater? → sunken
  5. Else (floating upright, intact, no distress) → good
  Note: a severely listing vessel is `capsized` UNLESS you can also see it is
  grounded, in which case rule 1 takes priority. Justify which applies.

Step 3 — Identify vessel type. Reason from visible features (hull shape,
superstructure, deck equipment, masts, cranes, armament) to a type.

Step 4 — Estimate size. Anchor your estimate to visible reference points:
people (~1.7m), standard doors/hatches (~2m), shipping containers (6m or 12m),
railings, vehicles, nearby vessels. Estimate length, then assign a category:
small (<10m), medium (10–50m), large (>50m). If no reference points are visible,
say so and give your best approximation.

Step 5 — Assess cargo. Note only cargo that is DIRECTLY VISIBLE (deck load,
containers, exposed bulk material). Do not infer cargo from the vessel type.
If none is visible, use null.

Step 6 — Assess surroundings. Describe the observable environment only: water
conditions, proximity to shore, weather, other vessels, visible hazards. Keep
operational/salvage implications out of this step.

Step 7 — Recovery considerations. Reason about the operational implications for
salvage: hull integrity, accessibility, estimated depth, structural damage, and
urgency indicators. (This is the "so what" of Step 6 — do not just repeat the
environment.)

Step 8 — Output. After your reasoning, output ONLY a valid JSON object with
exactly these fields and nothing else:
description, state, vessel_type, size_estimate, cargo, surroundings,
recovery_considerations.
